Medium Integral - Monday 12 October 2026

1. A lopsided curve on symmetric limits. Can a mirror even it out?
2. Thin rectangles estimate the area; watch the gap shrink.
3. Only e to the minus x changes under x tends to minus x; cos and cos h are even.
4. Reflecting the Variable
5. _ minus a to the a f(x) dx equals _ minus a to the a f(minus x) dx
6. Here f(minus x) equals e to the xcos xcos h x, since cos and cos h are even.
7. Mirror x tends to minus x: same limits, same area, now with e to the x.
8. Mirror the curve in the y-axis: the area stays the same.
9. Together they make 2cos x: the lean cancels out.
10. Add the original and its mirror: two copies of I.
11. Hyperbolic Cosine
12. cos h x equals e to the x plus e to the minus x2
13. So e to the minus x plus e to the x equals 2cos h x.
14. The bracket is exactly 2cos h x, so cos h x cancels.
15. What is left is the even curve 2cos x.
16. Integrate: 2cos x dx equals 2sine x.
17. 2sine pi over 2 minus 2sine (minus pi over 2) equals 2 plus 2 equals 4.
18. Halve it: I equals 2.
19. Thin rectangles agree: the lopsided area is exactly 2.
20. Takeaway: on symmetric limits, add f(x) and f(minus x).
